\subsection{Brazil confirmatory collection (14 models)}
\label{sec:results:bra}

The Brazil collection scores all 14 models across the five task types under both
persona conditions, with two stochastic replications, producing 2{,}024 valid
LLM-as-judge composites on the $[0,1]$ scale. It does not test the geographic
gradient (a single country), but it is the first run at full model coverage and
with the persona factor active.

\paragraph{Accuracy by model.} Table~\ref{tab:bra-model} orders the 14 models by
mean composite. The spread is wide (0.41 points), from DeepSeek-V3 at 0.713 to
the Brazilian-Portuguese regional model Cabra-Mistral~7B at 0.299. Frontier
systems (DeepSeek-V3, Gemini~2.5 Flash, GPT-5, GPT-5-mini, Claude Haiku) occupy
the top band; small open-weight models occupy the bottom. The result bearing on
H3 is striking and counter-directional: the regional Brazilian model is the
\emph{worst} performer on Brazilian air pollution policy, not the best. Regional
instruction tuning at the 7B scale does not buy domain accuracy here; it is
dominated by scale and by frontier training. This is a single-country, secondary
signal, but it directly contradicts the optimistic framing of H3a.

\paragraph{Accuracy by task and the regulatory-cutoff effect.}
Table~\ref{tab:bra-task} shows T1 (technical-standard recall) as the hardest task
by a wide margin (mean 0.369), below the open recommendation task T5 (0.489) and
well below the data and policy tasks (T2--T4, 0.60--0.67). The T1 result has a
concrete mechanism. Brazil's binding annual PM\textsubscript{2.5} standard moved
from the CONAMA Resolution 491/2018 framework to Resolution 506/2024 (the PI-2
stage in force in 2026). On the subset of T1 prompts scored against the
\emph{updated} 506/2024 ground truth, mean accuracy falls to roughly 0.16: models
overwhelmingly recall the superseded standard. Regulatory knowledge with a recent
cutoff is exactly where these systems fail, which is both a measurement caution
(ground truth must track the regulation in force) and a substantive finding for
public-sector use.

\paragraph{Persona effect (H6), paired within Brazil.} On the matched
neutral/persona prompt pairs (the same items asked under both framings, 118 pairs
across models and tasks), the public-environmental-manager persona moves mean
composite from 0.438 (neutral) to 0.446 (persona), a negligible $+0.8$ percentage
points. The per-task differences are small and mixed (T1 $-1.3$, T2 $+5.1$, T3
$-1.8$, T4 $+4.2$, T5 $-1.9$ pp). The persona frame neither narrows nor widens
accuracy materially in Brazil, consistent with evidence that expert personas do
not reliably improve factual accuracy \citep{zheng2024helpful,mollick2025personas}.
We caution that this is a within-country marginal contrast on a small paired
sample, not the country-level difference-in-differences of H6, which requires the
Global North/South contrast of the confirmatory study.

\paragraph{Open versus closed access (H5).} Grouping by access type, the
closed-accessible models average 0.666 against 0.513 for open-weight models, a
$+15.3$ percentage-point closed advantage. As in the pilot, this runs against H5;
unlike the pilot, the Brazil run includes larger Tier~A open models (Llama~3.3
70B, DeepSeek-V3), so the gap is less attributable to scale alone, though
DeepSeek-V3 itself leads all models. H5 remains exploratory and is interpreted
descriptively.

\subsection{Pilot design and scope}
\label{sec:results:pilot}

The Pilot~2.0 collection exercised the full scoring and analysis pipeline on a
reduced design: 7 countries (two Global North, five Global South), 5 of the
planned models, and English-language prompts across the five task types of
Section~\ref{sec:tasks}. Responses were scored by a single LLM judge (Claude
Haiku~4.5 under the G-Eval framework), yielding 700 judge scores on the
$[0,1]$ composite. This single-judge, single-language, small-$N$ configuration
is a deliberate feasibility exercise; the confirmatory protocol replaces it with
a multi-judge ensemble plus a human gold layer (Section~\ref{sec:methods}),
adds the persona factor, and scales to 15 countries. Because country-level cells
contain few observations, all pilot estimates are read as directional signals
rather than tests.

The composite by model is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:pilot-model}. Scores
span a 0.17-point range, from 0.668 for GPT-5-mini to 0.498 for Command~R+.
The two closed accessible API models (GPT-5-mini, Claude Haiku) and the closed
mid-tier Gemini Flash occupy the upper band, while the two open-weight models in
the pilot (Llama~4 Scout and Command~R+) fall below. We note that the judge
model (Claude Haiku~4.5) also appears in the evaluated set, a self-evaluation
overlap removed in the confirmatory design by using a non-overlapping judge.

\subsection{Composite accuracy by task}
\label{sec:results:task}

Table~\ref{tab:pilot-task} shows that difficulty varies sharply across the five
task types. The two hardest tasks are T1 (technical standard recall, mean 0.507)
and T5 (applied recommendation, mean 0.499), both with the widest dispersion.
The pattern is plausible. T1 requires recalling the specific binding ambient air
quality value in force, which in the Brazilian case has shifted with the
transition from the prior CONAMA Resolution 491/2018 framework to CONAMA
Resolution 506/2024 (the currently applicable PI-2 stage); recalling the precise
standard in force is exactly the kind of moving, jurisdiction-specific target
that strains model knowledge. T5 demands an open applied recommendation scored
against a rubric. The middle tasks T2 through T4 score higher and more tightly,
consistent with their more constrained answer spaces.

\subsection{Pilot signal for H1 (geographic gradient)}
\label{sec:results:h1}

Aggregating the five Global South countries against the two Global North
countries gives a Global South mean of 0.575 ($n=500$) versus a Global North
mean of 0.636 ($n=200$). The gap (Global North minus Global South) is
$+0.060$, or 6.0 percentage points, with a pooled SD of 0.180. The
corresponding pilot Cohen's $d$ is $+0.34$, a small-to-moderate effect. The
direction is consistent with H1: Global North outperforms Global South on this
air pollution policy task set. We treat this as a directional signal only. The
pilot is not powered for a country-level test, the prompts were English-only,
and the confirmatory study will estimate the gradient across 15 countries with
the multi-judge scoring and the formal Spearman and Mann-Kendall tests
pre-specified in Section~\ref{sec:methods}. The magnitude here informs the power
calibration rather than confirming the hypothesis.

\subsection{Pilot signal for H5 (open versus closed access)}
\label{sec:results:h5}

Grouping the pilot models by access type gives an open-frontier mean (Llama~4
Scout and Command~R+) of 0.517 ($n=280$) and a closed-accessible mean (Claude
Haiku, GPT-5-mini, Gemini Flash) of 0.643 ($n=420$). The gap is $+0.125$, or
12.5 percentage points, favouring the closed models. This is \emph{not}
consistent with H5 as framed: the open-weight models did not match the closed
models, and the gap well exceeds the 5-point threshold of interest. One caveat
is decisive for interpretation. The pilot used Llama~4 Scout at the 17B scale,
substantially smaller than the planned Tier~A open-weight frontier target
(70B-class and above). The open-versus-closed contrast in the pilot therefore
partly reflects a scale difference, not access type alone. The confirmatory
design includes larger Tier~A open models, which is the appropriate test of H5.

\subsection{Variance components}
\label{sec:results:variance}

A random-effects decomposition of the pilot composite (Table~\ref{tab:pilot-icc})
partitions variance across country, model, and residual sources. The
between-country intraclass correlation is small (ICC$_{\text{country}} = 0.031$),
the between-model component is larger (ICC$_{\text{model}} = 0.128$), and the
bulk of variance is residual within-cell (0.841). In the pilot, model identity
accounts for more variance than country of question, and most variation sits at
the individual-response level. These components feed directly into the Monte
Carlo power calibration of the pre-specified analysis plan
(Section~\ref{sec:methods}); the small country ICC in particular indicates that
detecting the geographic gradient will require the full 15-country sample and
the planned replication structure.

\subsection{H6 (persona): not tested in the pilot}
\label{sec:results:h6}

The persona manipulation (Section~\ref{sec:persona}) was \emph{not} exercised in
Pilot~2.0; all pilot prompts used the neutral framing. The persona factor was,
however, active in the Brazil confirmatory collection, where its paired
within-country effect on accuracy was negligible ($+0.8$ pp;
Section~\ref{sec:results:bra}), consistent with evidence that expert personas do
not reliably improve factual accuracy \citep{zheng2024helpful,mollick2025personas}.
The country-level difference-in-differences test of whether a public
environmental-manager frame narrows the \emph{geographic} gap still requires the
Global North/South contrast of the 15-country confirmatory study; the
single-country Brazil signal bears on whether persona changes accuracy at all,
not on whether it modulates the geographic gradient.

\subsection{Summary of pilot evidence}
\label{sec:results:summary}

Across both collections the pipeline runs end to end and produces interpretable
composite scores across models, countries, tasks, and persona conditions. Four
signals emerge. From the Brazil confirmatory run: the regional Brazilian model is
the weakest of the 14, contradicting the optimistic H3a framing; technical-standard
recall (T1) is the hardest task and collapses against the updated CONAMA 506/2024
ground truth, isolating a regulatory-cutoff failure mode; and the persona frame
has a negligible paired effect on accuracy ($+0.8$ pp), in line with prior work.
From the multi-country pilot: a 6.0-point Global North advantage consistent with
H1, and a closed-model advantage (12.5 pp in the pilot, 15.3 pp in Brazil) that
runs against H5. None of these signals are confirmatory, and the central
geographic test (H1) and persona-by-geography test (H6) both await the 15-country
collection. The geographic asymmetry on air pollution policy questions has direct
relevance for environmental management in the Global South, where the disease
burden is large \citep{gbd2019risk,requia2024shortterm} and where linguistic and
corpus under-representation \citep{joshi2020state,mohamed2020decolonial} plausibly
shapes model knowledge; quantifying it rigorously is the task of the planned
15-country confirmatory study.
