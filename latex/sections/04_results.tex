\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\begin{center}
\fbox{\parbox{0.88\linewidth}{%
\small
\textbf{Status note.} This is a \emph{15-country, 6-model, 4-language} benchmark.
The core design crosses 15 countries, 6 models spanning the five access tiers
(GPT-5, DeepSeek-V3, Claude Haiku, Qwen3-32B, Llama-3.1-8B, Cabra-Mistral-7B),

\subsection{Sample and judging}
\label{sec:results:sample}

The confirmatory set comprises the 150 air-pollution-policy prompts (15 countries
$\times$ 5 tasks $\times$ 2 personas) with two replications per model. After
excluding empty responses, the analysed corpus is 4{,}633 judge-scored responses
on the $[0,1]$ composite. The primary judge is GPT-5-mini. Because GPT-5-mini is
itself among the evaluated tier, an out-of-sample judge (Claude Sonnet~4.6, not
in the 14-model set) re-scored a stratified sample of 750 responses; agreement is
strong (Spearman $\rho = 0.87$, ICC(2,1) $= 0.82$, Pearson $r = 0.86$, mean
absolute difference $0.11$), which neutralizes the judge--target overlap for the
reported composites (Section~\ref{sec:results:reliability}).

\subsection{Accuracy by model}
\label{sec:results:model}

Table~\ref{tab:conf-model} orders the 13 models by mean composite. Frontier
systems (GPT-5, DeepSeek-V3, GPT-5-mini) lead; small open-weight models trail. The
result bearing on H3 replicates the pilot and the Brazil collection: the
Brazilian-Portuguese regional model Cabra-Mistral~7B is the \emph{weakest} of all
13 (0.308 versus 0.524 for the rest; Mann-Whitney $p<10^{-300}$, Cliff's
$\delta=-0.48$), contradicting the optimistic H3a framing that a regional model
buys domain accuracy. Scale and frontier training dominate regional instruction
tuning at the 7B level.

\subsection{Accuracy by task: the factual-recall floor}
\label{sec:results:task}

Difficulty is sharply task-dependent (Table~\ref{tab:conf-task}). The two
hardest tasks are \textbf{T1 (technical-standard recall, 0.319)} and \textbf{T2
(local measured datum, 0.316)} --- precisely the tasks that require retrieving a
specific binding value (a national annual PM\textsubscript{2.5} standard; a
city-year concentration). The open recommendation task (T5) scores highest
(0.805) because its rubric rewards plausible, well-structured policy advice that
does not hinge on a single fact. The two factual-recall tasks (T1, T2, pooled
mean 0.317) score far below the synthesis and recommendation tasks (T3--T5,
0.641); the difference is large and unambiguous (Mann-Whitney $p<10^{-300}$,
Cliff's $\delta=-0.69$). This is the most robust finding in the study:
LLMs are weakest exactly where applied environmental management needs them most
--- recalling the precise regulatory threshold or measured value in force.

\subsection{H1 --- a tier gap, but not a development gradient}
\label{sec:results:h1}

H1 was pre-specified in two parts, and the data separate them cleanly. The
\textbf{tier gap} holds: the three Global North countries average 0.562 against
0.497 for the twelve Global South countries, a gap of \textbf{+6.7 percentage
points} whose 95\% bootstrap confidence interval over countries is
$[+2.7, +10.3]$~pp and \emph{excludes zero} (Cohen's $d=+0.25$), replicating the
pilot. But the \textbf{monotonic gradient} the protocol pre-specified ---
accuracy increasing with the linguistic-resource and development axes --- does
\emph{not} hold: country-level Spearman correlations are essentially null for HDI
($\rho=+0.16$, $p=0.29$) and for Joshi class ($\rho=-0.01$), and the Mann-Kendall
trend by HDI is non-significant ($p=0.69$). The country ordering
(Table~\ref{tab:conf-country}) shows why: India (0.632) tops all countries and
Indonesia (0.539, both Global South) outscore Germany (0.531, Global North),
while Argentina and Brazil --- both relatively high-HDI Global South ---
anchor the bottom. The geographic disadvantage is therefore real at the tier
level but is \emph{not} a smooth function of national development or linguistic
resource: it is a binary North/South gap layered over large, axis-orthogonal
country heterogeneity.

\subsection{H4 --- corpus representation is not the mechanism}
\label{sec:results:h4}

The pre-specified mechanism for the geographic gap was training-corpus
representation, proxied at the country level by the article count of the
dominant official language's Wikipedia (Wikimedia, 2026), with HDI (UNDP HDR
2023--24) as the developmental control. The association is null: Spearman
$\rho(\text{accuracy}, \log\text{Wikipedia}) = +0.14$ ($p=0.63$), and the partial
correlation controlling for HDI is $+0.17$ ($p=0.55$). \textbf{H4 is not
supported}: at the country level, neither corpus size nor development accounts
for the accuracy differences. The gap is not explained by the obvious resource
mechanisms, which sharpens rather than weakens the result --- the geographic
disadvantage is not simply ``less data for poorer or less-online countries.''
We treat this proxy as coarse (it does not separate countries that share an
official language) and revisit a finer per-country corpus measure as future work.

\subsection{H6 --- persona does not narrow the geographic gap}
\label{sec:results:h6}

The persona factor was fully crossed in this collection, so H6 can be tested as a
difference-in-differences. The public-environmental-manager frame moves Global
South accuracy by $\Delta_{\mathrm{GS}} = -0.1$ pp and Global North accuracy by
$\Delta_{\mathrm{GN}} = -0.8$ pp, giving a DiD of $\Delta_{\mathrm{GS}} -
\Delta_{\mathrm{GN}} = \textbf{+0.6 pp}$ --- far below the pre-specified 5 pp
threshold, and a country-level permutation test (5{,}000 permutations of the
tier label) gives $p=0.24$. The geographic gap is essentially unchanged by
persona: +6.8 pp under the neutral frame versus +6.2 pp under the manager frame.
\textbf{H6 is not supported}: presenting the query as coming from a public
environmental manager neither narrows nor amplifies the Global South disadvantage.
This is consistent with evidence that expert personas do not reliably improve
factual accuracy~\citep{zheng2024helpful,mollick2025personas}, and it answers the
study's central applied question in the negative --- prompt framing is not a fix
for geographic bias.

\subsection{H5 --- open versus closed-accessible}
\label{sec:results:h5}

Grouping by access type, closed-accessible models average 0.604 ($n=868$) against
0.461 for open-weight models ($n=3{,}185$), a +14.3 pp closed advantage. As in the
pilot, this runs against H5; here the open arm includes larger Tier~A models
(Llama~3.3 70B, DeepSeek-V3), so the gap is less attributable to scale alone,
although DeepSeek-V3 itself ranks second overall. H5 is interpreted descriptively.

\subsection{H2 --- native-language prompting lowers accuracy}
\label{sec:results:h2}

The 4-language arm renders each prompt for the five countries with a target
native language (Brazil~$\to$~Portuguese, Mexico/Argentina/Peru~$\to$~Spanish,
India~$\to$~Hindi) in both English and the native language, holding content
fixed, so the English/native contrast is within-country and within-model.
Across the matched (model, prompt) pairs, native-language prompting
\emph{lowers} mean composite accuracy by 3.5~percentage points (English 0.506
$\to$ native 0.471; Wilcoxon signed-rank $p=5\times10^{-4}$). The effect is
strongly modulated by the resource level of the native language
(Table~\ref{tab:h2}): for the high-resource Latin-script languages the penalty is
small and not individually significant (Spanish $-1.8$~pp, $p=0.07$; Portuguese
$-0.5$~pp, $p=0.87$), but for Hindi it is large and highly significant
($-11.6$~pp, $p=1.7\times10^{-4}$). India, which scores highest of all countries in
English (0.646), drops to 0.529 in Hindi. The applied implication mirrors H6:
the intuitive fix --- letting a local manager ask in their own language --- does
not help and, for a non-Latin-script lower-resource language, materially hurts.
This is consistent with the linguistic-resource hierarchy~\citep{joshi2020state}
and motivates the corpus-representation mechanism (H4).

\subsection{Ground-truth provenance}
\label{sec:results:gt}

Because measured ``accuracy'' depends on the ground truth, the technical-standard
target (T1) for all 15 countries was anchored to official primary sources by
documentary verification rather than expert assertion, and is reproducible from
the cited URLs. Eleven countries have a value read from the official regulation
(e.g.\ Brazil's annual PM\textsubscript{2.5} standard of 17~$\mu$g/m\textsuperscript{3}
read verbatim from Annex~I of CONAMA Resolution 506/2024 in the Di\'ario Oficial
da Uni\~ao). Two countries have \emph{no} national PM\textsubscript{2.5} standard,
confirmed from the official text --- Nigeria's NESREA regulation sets PM10 only,
and Argentina has no binding federal value --- a fact the audit established
against a circulating but incorrect secondary figure. Two (Bangladesh, Egypt)
have the governing law identified with the numeric value partially confirmed.
This provenance is more reproducible than expert validation: any reader can
re-verify each value from the official source.

\subsection{Inter-judge reliability}
\label{sec:results:reliability}

To address the judge--target overlap (GPT-5-mini is in the evaluated set), an
out-of-sample judge (Claude Sonnet~4.6) re-scored a stratified random sample of
750 responses spanning all countries, tasks, and personas. Agreement on the
composite is strong: Pearson $r = 0.861$, Spearman $\rho = 0.874$, ICC(2,1)
absolute agreement $= 0.824$, mean absolute difference $0.112$. An ICC above 0.80
indicates good-to-excellent reliability and supports treating the GPT-5-mini
composites as judge-independent for the effects reported here. The final
confirmatory analysis will replace the single judge with the pre-specified
multi-judge ensemble plus a human gold layer.

\subsection{Summary}
\label{sec:results:summary}

The formal tests draw a clean line between what is supported and what is not.
\textbf{Supported, with effect sizes:} a Global North/South \emph{tier} gap
(+6.7 pp, bootstrap 95\% CI $[+2.7,+10.3]$, excludes zero); a native-language
\emph{penalty} (overall $p=5\times10^{-4}$, Hindi $p=1.7\times10^{-4}$); a
technical-/local-recall \emph{floor} (T1+T2 vs others, $p<10^{-300}$,
$\delta=-0.69$); and the regional model as the \emph{worst} performer
($p<10^{-300}$, $\delta=-0.48$). \textbf{Not supported (informative nulls):} the
geographic effect is \emph{not} a monotonic gradient in development or linguistic
resource (Spearman vs HDI $\rho=0.16$, vs Joshi $\rho=-0.01$); corpus
representation is \emph{not} the mechanism (partial $\rho=0.17$, $p=0.55$); and
persona does \emph{not} narrow the gap (permutation $p=0.24$). Under Bonferroni-Holm
across the primary family $\{$H1-gradient, H4, H6$\}$, none of the
gradient/mechanism tests reach significance. The story is therefore disciplined
and non-obvious: the geographic disadvantage is real but modest and \emph{not}
explained by the usual development or corpus accounts; the dominant, robust harms
are \emph{linguistic} (native-language prompting, worst for Hindi) and
\emph{task-specific} (factual recall of the regulation or datum in force); and all
three intuitive remedies --- a local-manager persona, the local language, and a
regional model --- fail. The remaining open item is the multi-judge ensemble plus
human gold layer; ground truth is anchored to official primary sources for all 15
countries, and reliability against an out-of-sample judge is strong (ICC 0.82).
This matters for environmental management in the Global South, where the disease
burden is large~\citep{gbd2019risk,requia2024shortterm} and where exactly the
hardest tasks --- recalling the binding standard or measured value --- are the
ones a manager most needs~\citep{joshi2020state,mohamed2020decolonial}.
