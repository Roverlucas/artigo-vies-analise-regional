\subsection{Pilot design and scope}
\label{sec:results:pilot}

The Pilot~2.0 collection exercised the full scoring and analysis pipeline on a
reduced design: 7 countries (two Global North, five Global South), 5 of the
planned models, and English-language prompts across the five task types of
Section~\ref{sec:tasks}. Responses were scored by a single LLM judge (Claude
Haiku~4.5 under the G-Eval framework), yielding 700 judge scores on the
$[0,1]$ composite. This single-judge, single-language, small-$N$ configuration
is a deliberate feasibility exercise; the confirmatory protocol replaces it with
a multi-judge ensemble plus a human gold layer (Section~\ref{sec:methods}),
adds the persona factor, and scales to 15 countries. Because country-level cells
contain few observations, all pilot estimates are read as directional signals
rather than tests.

The composite by model is summarized in Table~\ref{tab:pilot-model}. Scores
span a 0.17-point range, from 0.668 for GPT-5-mini to 0.498 for Command~R+.
The two closed accessible API models (GPT-5-mini, Claude Haiku) and the closed
mid-tier Gemini Flash occupy the upper band, while the two open-weight models in
the pilot (Llama~4 Scout and Command~R+) fall below. We note that the judge
model (Claude Haiku~4.5) also appears in the evaluated set, a self-evaluation
overlap removed in the confirmatory design by using a non-overlapping judge.

\subsection{Composite accuracy by task}
\label{sec:results:task}

Table~\ref{tab:pilot-task} shows that difficulty varies sharply across the five
task types. The two hardest tasks are T1 (technical standard recall, mean 0.507)
and T5 (applied recommendation, mean 0.499), both with the widest dispersion.
The pattern is plausible. T1 requires recalling the specific binding ambient air
quality value in force, which in the Brazilian case has shifted with the
transition from the prior CONAMA Resolution 491/2018 framework to CONAMA
Resolution 506/2024 (the currently applicable PI-2 stage); recalling the precise
standard in force is exactly the kind of moving, jurisdiction-specific target
that strains model knowledge. T5 demands an open applied recommendation scored
against a rubric. The middle tasks T2 through T4 score higher and more tightly,
consistent with their more constrained answer spaces.

\subsection{Pilot signal for H1 (geographic gradient)}
\label{sec:results:h1}

Aggregating the five Global South countries against the two Global North
countries gives a Global South mean of 0.575 ($n=500$) versus a Global North
mean of 0.636 ($n=200$). The gap (Global North minus Global South) is
$+0.060$, or 6.0 percentage points, with a pooled SD of 0.180. The
corresponding pilot Cohen's $d$ is $+0.34$, a small-to-moderate effect. The
direction is consistent with H1: Global North outperforms Global South on this
air pollution policy task set. We treat this as a directional signal only. The
pilot is not powered for a country-level test, the prompts were English-only,
and the confirmatory study will estimate the gradient across 15 countries with
the multi-judge scoring and the formal Spearman and Mann-Kendall tests
pre-specified in Section~\ref{sec:methods}. The magnitude here informs the power
calibration rather than confirming the hypothesis.

\subsection{Pilot signal for H5 (open versus closed access)}
\label{sec:results:h5}

Grouping the pilot models by access type gives an open-frontier mean (Llama~4
Scout and Command~R+) of 0.517 ($n=280$) and a closed-accessible mean (Claude
Haiku, GPT-5-mini, Gemini Flash) of 0.643 ($n=420$). The gap is $+0.125$, or
12.5 percentage points, favouring the closed models. This is \emph{not}
consistent with H5 as framed: the open-weight models did not match the closed
models, and the gap well exceeds the 5-point threshold of interest. One caveat
is decisive for interpretation. The pilot used Llama~4 Scout at the 17B scale,
substantially smaller than the planned Tier~A open-weight frontier target
(70B-class and above). The open-versus-closed contrast in the pilot therefore
partly reflects a scale difference, not access type alone. The confirmatory
design includes larger Tier~A open models, which is the appropriate test of H5.

\subsection{Variance components}
\label{sec:results:variance}

A random-effects decomposition of the pilot composite (Table~\ref{tab:pilot-icc})
partitions variance across country, model, and residual sources. The
between-country intraclass correlation is small (ICC$_{\text{country}} = 0.031$),
the between-model component is larger (ICC$_{\text{model}} = 0.128$), and the
bulk of variance is residual within-cell (0.841). In the pilot, model identity
accounts for more variance than country of question, and most variation sits at
the individual-response level. These components feed directly into the Monte
Carlo power calibration of the pre-specified analysis plan
(Section~\ref{sec:methods}); the small country ICC in particular indicates that
detecting the geographic gradient will require the full 15-country sample and
the planned replication structure.

\subsection{H6 (persona): not tested in the pilot}
\label{sec:results:h6}

The persona manipulation (Section~\ref{sec:persona}) was \emph{not} exercised in
Pilot~2.0. All pilot prompts used the neutral framing, so there are no pilot data
bearing on H6. The difference-in-differences test of whether a public
environmental-manager role frame narrows the geographic gap will be collected and
analysed only in the confirmatory study. We flag here that prior work finds
persona or role prompts do not reliably improve task accuracy
\citep{zheng2024helpful,mollick2025personas}, which makes H6 a genuinely open
question rather than a foregone conclusion; the pilot offers no evidence either
way.

\subsection{Summary of pilot evidence}
\label{sec:results:summary}

The pilot demonstrates that the collection, scoring, and analysis pipeline runs
end to end and produces interpretable composite scores across models, countries,
and tasks. Two directional signals emerge: a 6.0-point Global North advantage
consistent with H1, and a 12.5-point closed-model advantage that runs against
H5 but is confounded by the small open model used. The variance decomposition
points to model identity and residual variation dominating country effects at
this scale. None of these signals are confirmatory. The geographic asymmetry on
air pollution policy questions has direct relevance for environmental management
in the Global South, where the disease burden is large
\citep{gbd2019risk,requia2024shortterm} and where linguistic and corpus
under-representation \citep{joshi2020state,mohamed2020decolonial} plausibly
shapes model knowledge; quantifying it rigorously is the task of the planned
15-country confirmatory study, not of this pilot.
