\begin{abstract}

Public officials increasingly consult large language models (LLMs) about the
regulations they are responsible for applying, and generative AI is already in
routine administrative use~\citep{kim2026genai}. When the answer is wrong, the
official carries the consequence~\citep{choroszewicz2026crumple}, and
technology-neutral regulation does not reach the
failure~\citep{cordella2024regulating}, which leaves verification at the point of
use as the operative control. Whether that reliability is distributed evenly
across the states that depend on it is unmeasured, because existing evaluations
score perception, explanation quality or generic factual recall rather than
agreement with the binding text.

We test this where the correct answer is published in an official register and
can be checked: environmental regulation. No prior benchmark combines a regulated
policy domain, official-source ground truth, a pre-specified persona factor, and
a mechanism test that separates language-corpus size from country coverage. The
design spans
\textbf{25 countries, 14 LLMs, four languages, and five task types}
(normative recall, local measured data, health-evidence synthesis, policy
instruments, and applied recommendation). We
evaluate each model \emph{as served} through a fixed access stack (vendor API or
pinned inference endpoint), with a within-subject persona factor (neutral versus
public environmental manager) and a within-country English/native-language
contrast. The design was pre-specified for 15 countries and then extended post
hoc to 25 (seven more Global North, three more Global South) to gain power; every
effect is reported alongside its pre-specified 15-country value.

Three harms are robust. A Global North/South \textbf{tier} gap of $+6.2$~pp
(bootstrap 95\% CI $[+3.7,+8.6]$, excludes zero) resolves, at $n=25$, into a
\textbf{development gradient} (Spearman $\rho=0.51$ with HDI, $p=0.004$;
Mann-Kendall $p=0.018$; leave-one-out robust) that was undetectable at the
pre-specified $n=15$ ($\rho=0.14$), sits just below our pre-specified $0.55$
effect-size threshold, and tracks development, \emph{not} linguistic-resource
class (Joshi null). \textbf{Native-language prompting lowers accuracy}
($-2.1$~pp overall, $p=2\times10^{-3}$; $-7.8$~pp for Hindi), so the local
language does not help and can hurt. \textbf{Accuracy collapses on
technical-standard and local-datum recall} (T1, T2 $\approx 0.37$ vs $0.64$
elsewhere; Cliff's $\delta=-0.64$), a regulatory-cutoff floor; and the
Brazilian-Portuguese \textbf{regional model is the weakest of all}
($\delta=-0.51$). Three intuitive fixes therefore fail together: neither a
local-manager persona (permutation $p=0.26$), nor the local language, nor a
regional model closes the gap.

On mechanism, the pre-specified corpus proxy (Wikipedia size) is null; a
country-coverage proxy (Wikidata sitelinks) correlates with accuracy
($\rho=0.54$, $p=0.005$, surviving multiplicity correction) but attenuates to
non-significance after adjusting for development ($p=0.13$). We therefore report
country coverage as a \emph{suggestive, exploratory} channel --- better than
language-corpus size, as expected for a gap measured in English --- not as an
established mechanism. The headline effects are stable across three composite
weightings, a crossed-effects mixed model, and a Bayesian re-estimation, with
E-values up to $5.4$. Reliability across a four-vendor judge panel is strong
(panel-mean ICC$(2,4)=0.89$), and ground truth is anchored to official primary
sources for all 25 countries; the study uses one panel-validated primary judge
and a documentary ground-truth audit. Beyond its findings, the study offers a
reusable, fully reproducible template --- official-source ground truth,
multi-method robustness, and an end-to-end audit pipeline --- for applied
LLM-equity evaluation. Protocol, prompts, and analysis code will be released on
publication.

\end{abstract}
