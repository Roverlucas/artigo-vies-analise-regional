\begin{abstract}

Public environmental managers increasingly delegate air pollution policy
questions to large language models (LLMs), yet whether these systems answer as
reliably for the Global South as for the Global North, and whether prompt
framing changes the answer, remains unmeasured for this high-stakes domain.
PM\textsubscript{2.5} is among the leading environmental risk factors for
premature mortality, and the burden falls disproportionately on the Global
South, where corpus and linguistic under-representation plausibly shape model
knowledge~\citep{gbd2019risk,joshi2020state,mohamed2020decolonial}. We benchmark large
language models in a single applied domain, air pollution policy, anchored to
official ground truth across five task types (normative recall, local measured
data, health-evidence synthesis, policy instruments, and applied recommendation),
crossing 15 theoretically stratified countries, 13 of 14 planned LLMs across five
access tiers, and a pre-specified persona factor (neutral versus public
environmental manager) tested within subject. From 4{,}633 LLM-as-judge composite
scores we find: (i) a small but consistent geographic gradient --- the Global
North outscores the Global South by 6.5 percentage points (Cohen's $d=+0.25$),
replicating a pilot signal (H1); (ii) persona prompting does \emph{not} narrow
that gap (difference-in-differences $+0.6$ pp, below the 5 pp threshold), so
prompt framing is not a fix for geographic bias (H6 not supported), consistent
with evidence that expert personas do not improve factual
accuracy~\citep{zheng2024helpful,mollick2025personas}; (iii) the
Brazilian-Portuguese regional model is the weakest of all 13, contradicting the
optimistic regional-model hypothesis (H3); and (iv) failure concentrates in
technical-standard and local-datum recall ($\approx 0.32$), a regulatory-cutoff
failure mode. Inter-judge reliability against an out-of-sample judge is strong
(ICC $=0.82$). The collection is confirmatory-partial: it excludes one model for
lack of vendor credit, runs in English only, and uses a single judge; the
corpus-mechanism test (H4), the multilingual matrix (H2), and expert validation
of all-country ground truth are deferred to the final analysis. Protocol,
prompts, and analysis code will be released on publication.

\end{abstract}
