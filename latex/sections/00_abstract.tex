\begin{abstract}

Public environmental managers increasingly delegate air pollution policy
questions to large language models (LLMs), yet whether these systems answer as
reliably for the Global South as for the Global North, and whether prompt
framing changes the answer, remains unmeasured for this high-stakes domain.
PM\textsubscript{2.5} is among the leading environmental risk factors for
premature mortality, and the burden falls disproportionately on the Global
South, where corpus and linguistic under-representation plausibly shape model
knowledge~\citep{gbd2019risk,joshi2020state,mohamed2020decolonial}. We present a
study protocol and pilot evidence for a benchmark in a single applied domain,
air pollution policy, anchored to verifiable official ground truth across five
task types (normative recall, local measured data, health-evidence synthesis,
policy instruments, and applied recommendation). The planned confirmatory study
crosses 15 theoretically stratified countries, 14 LLMs spanning five access
tiers, and a pre-specified persona factor (neutral versus public environmental
manager) tested within subject. Three co-primary hypotheses are pre-specified:
a geographic accuracy gradient (H1), a corpus-representation mechanism (H4), and
a persona effect on the Global South gap (H6); a regional model probe
(Cabra-Mistral~7B) is secondary (H3). Because the persona literature finds that
role prompts do not reliably improve factual
accuracy~\citep{zheng2024helpful,mollick2025personas}, H6 is treated as an open
question with both reduction and amplification pre-specified. A reduced pilot (7
countries, 5 models, English prompts, no persona) yields a directional signal:
the Global North outscored the Global South by 6 percentage points (Cohen's
$d=+0.34$), consistent with H1. The 15-country confirmatory study and the
persona manipulation are planned, not yet executed. The full protocol, prompts,
and analysis code will be released on publication.

\end{abstract}
