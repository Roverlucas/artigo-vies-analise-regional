\section{Pre-specification, design, and country selection}
% ============================================================

The hypotheses, primary models, persona protocol, exclusion criteria,
multiple-comparison corrections, and the country sampling frame were specified in
advance of confirmatory data collection. The pre-specified design covers
15 countries; the sample was subsequently extended, \emph{post
registration}, to 25 countries to gain power for the development gradient
(H1) and the corpus-mechanism tests (H4) and to multiply the within-country
language pairs (H2). Throughout the manuscript and this supplement, every effect
computed on the 25-country sample is reported alongside its pre-specified
15-country value, and no pre-specified conclusion is revised on the basis of the
extension alone (Section~\ref{sec:s-stats}).

Countries were selected by theoretical sampling triangulated across three
independent classifications: the Global North/South distinction, taken from
UNCTAD's developed/developing economies classification
(operationalising the coloniality-of-knowledge framing), the Joshi et al.\ (2020)
six-class linguistic-resource taxonomy~\citep{joshi2020state}, and World Bank
income groups. The pre-specified 15 (Set ``P'' in Table~\ref{tab:s-cov-country})
span four world regions, five Joshi classes, and four income groups; the 10
extension countries (Set ``E'') comprise six additional Global North references
(United Kingdom, Canada, Australia, South Korea, France, Italy) chosen to thicken
the high-development end of the gradient, and four additional native-language-pair
countries (Colombia, Chile, Angola, Portugal), of which Portugal is Global North;
the extension therefore adds seven Global North and three Global South countries. UNCTAD states that
developed economies ``broadly comprise Northern America and Europe, Israel, Japan,
the Republic of Korea, Australia, and New Zealand'', developing economies being the
remainder; all 25 countries fall on the side that rule assigns them, South Korea
included. The full design rationale is in
\texttt{docs/supplement/s1\_design\_rationale.md}.

\section{Deployment-stack roster and serving configuration}
% ============================================================

\paragraph{Unit of analysis.} We evaluate each model \emph{as served} through a
fixed access stack --- the tuple of model weights, vendor, serving infrastructure,
and API/endpoint layer --- rather than idealized weights in isolation, because a
public manager interacts with the served stack and any measured gap is a property
of that stack. Table~\ref{tab:s-roster} lists the 14 stacks with their exact
API/tag strings and execution venues.

\input{supplement/tables/tab_s_roster}

\paragraph{Determinism and seed handling.} All stacks were queried at
temperature~0.3 with the maximum-determinism configuration each provider
documents, not an identical parameter surface, because the surfaces differ. Seed
support is heterogeneous: locally served stacks (Ollama) honour a fixed seed;
OpenAI exposes an advisory seed; Anthropic's Messages API exposes no seed
parameter; and for the remaining hosted endpoints a seed is either advisory or
not exposed. For every call we record the requested seed and a
\texttt{seed\_status} field (\texttt{sent}, \texttt{logged-only}, or
\texttt{not-supported}) in the released run metadata, so that the determinism
actually achieved is auditable rather than assumed. Two stochastic replications
per (prompt, model, persona) cell estimate within-cell variability.

\paragraph{Acquisition window.} Served-model behaviour can drift as vendors update
infrastructure, so the reported accuracies are point-in-time estimates relative to
the collection window. The pinned tag, version string, and acquisition timestamp
are recorded for every call. Confirmatory collection ran from
2026-04-26 to 2026-06-12 (UTC), spanning the pilot, the pre-specified
15-country collection, and the post-hoc extension to 25 countries.
Table~\ref{tab:s-acquisition} gives the first and last non-error call timestamp
per provider together with the official API documentation consulted; the full
per-call timestamps are released with the dataset.

\section{Country covariates}
% ============================================================

Table~\ref{tab:s-covariates} reports the developmental and corpus-representation
covariates used in the mechanism analysis (H4). Language-corpus measures
(Joshi class; mC4/OSCAR sizes reported in the main text) index how much text
exists \emph{in} a country's language and are the candidate channel for the
native-language penalty (H2). Country-coverage measures (English-Wikipedia
article byte length, Wikidata sitelinks, Wikidata statements) index how broadly
the encyclopedic corpus is \emph{about} a country, independent of language, and
are the candidate channel for the geographic gap (H1/H4). Because the geographic
gap is measured \emph{in English}, a residual North/South gap cannot be a
language-corpus effect, which is why H4 isolates country coverage for H1 and
language-corpus size for H2. All Wikidata entities are identified by a resolvable
QID and were retrieved within the collection window.

\section{Ground-truth registry (T1)}
% ============================================================

The validity of the geographic gradient (H1) requires that ground truth be
equally available and equally verifiable across countries. For T1 (the binding
annual PM\textsubscript{2.5} standard) we anchored every country to an official
primary source by documentary audit: the source text was retrieved, the value was
read verbatim, and the entry records the value, status, source-of-record, a
resolvable URL, and the retrieval method (Table~\ref{tab:s-groundtruth}). Three
countries have \emph{no} national PM\textsubscript{2.5} standard, confirmed from
the official text (Nigeria's NESREA regulates PM\textsubscript{10} only; Argentina
has no binding federal value; Angola has no national air-quality legislation); in
those cases a faithful model must decline to state a non-existent threshold rather
than fabricate one. This provenance is more reproducible than expert assertion:
any reader can re-verify each value from the cited official source. One entry has no
value: Egypt, whose official Arabic text could not be retrieved, so nothing is
stored and Egypt is excluded from T1. Angola's no-standard status rests
on a World Bank/WHO country note rather than on a national instrument, because no
national air-quality instrument exists to cite. Every other value was confirmed
against the primary text. The United Kingdom entry was corrected on 2026-09-21
from 10 (the English 2040 target) to 20~$\mu$g/m\textsuperscript{3}, the limit
value in force since 2020, after checking DEFRA's national objectives table; and
the Bangladesh entry was corrected the same day from 15 (S.R.O. 220-Law/2005) to
35~$\mu$g/m\textsuperscript{3} (Air Pollution (Control) Rules 2022, S.R.O.
255-Law/2022), after an external reader flagged the revision. Because the
registry moved under scrutiny, every number in the manuscript is tied to a
registry version: the released files carry SHA-256 prefixes
\texttt{t1\_registry} b0e5d9e132aab684, \texttt{t2\_registry} 703dcd8122f88448, \texttt{t3\_registry} 902de1bc6af893de,
\texttt{t4\_reference\_set} 77bca4679aedc93a, and the analysis pipeline prints them.

\section{Composite outcome and scoring rubric}
% ============================================================

Every response carries five subcomponents each normalised to $[0,1]$. The factual
subcomponent is decided by code where the registry holds a value (T1, and the
resolvable part of T2 and T3); the three-vendor panel scores the residual of T2
and T3 and all of T4; T5 keeps the scores of the collection judge (GPT-5-mini for
the pre-specified fifteen countries, Claude Haiku~4.5 for the ten added in the
extension). The subcomponents are:
\begin{enumerate}\setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \item Factual accuracy against the registry value (binary for T1;
        partial credit for T2--T4).
  \item Contextual completeness scored against a pre-validated rubric
        (T3, T4).
  \item Citation quality, verified via DOI/official-source check.
  \item Calibration, the alignment between stated confidence and correctness.
  \item Absence of hallucination, coded binarily ($0$ hallucinated,
        $1$ faithful).
\end{enumerate}
The primary composite uses a pre-specified weighting (factual~0.30,
contextual~0.25, citation~0.15, calibration~0.15, hallucination~0.15). As a
sensitivity analysis the headline effects are recomputed under two alternative
weightings --- equal weights and a PCA-derived set (the first principal component
of the five subcomponents) --- and a conclusion is treated as robust only if its
direction holds under all three; every headline effect keeps its sign under every
weighting (Table~\ref{tab:s-weighting}). The composite is computed per (country, model,
persona) cell to support the H6 difference-in-differences test. Each scored record
also stores the judge's free-text rationale; for example, the
\texttt{gpt-5-mini} judge scored a Llama~4~Scout reply on Brazil's T1 standard
$0.15$ with the rationale that it ``gives wrong regulation (491/2018 vs correct
506/2024) and wrong current value\ldots\ included a specific but incorrect
citation and fabricated timing,'' illustrating how the factual-accuracy and
hallucination subcomponents discriminate substantive failure.

\paragraph{Mixed model and Bayesian re-estimation.} A Gaussian linear mixed model
(\texttt{statsmodels} MixedLM, REML) with crossed random intercepts for country and
model gives a Global South effect of $\beta=\nmixedbeta$ ($\mathrm{SE}=\nmixedse$,
$p=\nmixedp$), marginal rather than conventionally significant because it charges the
large between-country heterogeneity to the error term. A Bayesian hierarchical
re-estimation with weakly informative priors (\texttt{pymc}; crossed country/model
intercepts, \texttt{random\_seed=42}) agrees on magnitude and is decisive about the
sign: posterior mean $\nbayes$, 94\% HDI $[\nbayeslo,\nbayeshi]$, $P(\beta<0)=\nbayesp$
($\hat{R}=\nrhat$).

\paragraph{E-values.} The tier gap carries an E-value of $\nevalue$ at the point
estimate and $\nevaluelim$ at the confidence limit nearest the null (VanderWeele \& Ding
2017 approximation from the standardized effect); the second is the operative
number, and it is a low bar. No E-value is reported for the development gradient:
its interval already includes the null, so the E-value at that limit is $1.00$ and
no confounder at all is required.

\paragraph{Exploratory mediation (H4).} A standardized country-level path model
(\texttt{semopy}, $n=25$): $\mathrm{HDI}\rightarrow$ sitelinks $a=\nmedab$;
sitelinks $\rightarrow$ accuracy $b=\nmedb$; direct $c'=\nmedc$; indirect
$a b=\nmedabx$ with bootstrap 95\% CI $[\nmedlo,\nmedhi]$ (includes zero). Country
coverage is thus a suggestive but not established mediator, consistent with the
main-text framing.

\paragraph{Persona manipulation check.} Across 4{,}349 persona-condition responses,
$\nmanip\%$ explicitly acknowledge the public-manager role frame, so the null persona
effect (H6) is not an artifact of the frame being universally ignored.

A further property of the response corpus, not a departure from the plan but a
consequence of how collection was retried, belongs here because it bears on
reproducibility. Of \num{9760} unique (model, prompt, replicate) keys, \num{2147}
(22.0\%) have more than one stored response, produced by re-runs on different
dates; restricting to records the scoring pipeline admits gives \num{9140} keys
and \num{2095} duplicates (22.9\%). Earlier drafts reported 4{,}976 and 1{,}003
here, figures that predate the extension to 25 countries and that we could not
reproduce from the released artefacts; the counts above are regenerated by
\texttt{code/analysis/robustness\_extra.py}. The
stored answers differ substantially between runs; in one case the same prompt and
model yielded 307 and \num{3180} characters. The answer that enters the analysis is the first stored in run-file order, which
is the one the collection judge scored (the judge runner reads the run files in
order and skips keys already scored); the code verdicts for T1, T2 and T3 are
computed on that same answer, so both instruments describe one response. Scoring
every stored answer and averaging the verdicts instead changes the code verdict
in \ndupchange\% of cells and the mean factual score by \ndupmean
(\texttt{code/analysis/t1\_and\_wave\_sensitivity.py}). Re-judging under a
chronological rule would require re-running the panel and was not done.

\emph{Extractor audit for T1.} All ten native-language T1 responses that the
extractor classified as carrying no comparable value were read one by one
(\texttt{data/processed/t1\_extractor\_audit.md}). None contains an annual
PM\textsubscript{2.5} value in $\mu$g/m\textsuperscript{3} that the extractor
missed: seven are degenerate outputs of the regional 7B model (repeated prompt,
nonsense text), two cite the regulation without a number, and one asks
permission to look the value up; two further responses state ``15 mg/m\textsuperscript{3}'',
a value in the wrong unit, which is treated as no comparable value in every
language. The extractor's handling of decimal commas, Devanagari numerals and
units written in words is fixed by 13 unit tests released with the code. The
audit was performed by the analysis pipeline's author rather than by an
independent reader, and is recorded as such.

Prompts were authored in English for the air pollution policy domain with domain
supervision (PPGSAU/UTFPR) and validated against the ground-truth registry before
entering the confirmatory stimulus set. The plan required a two-rater pilot to
reach Krippendorff's $\alpha\geq 0.70$ before scaling; that pilot was not run,
and the deviation is logged in Section~\ref{sec:s-deviations}. Native-language
renderings (Portuguese, Spanish, Hindi) were produced by a high-quality LLM
translator (Claude Sonnet~4.6) instructed to preserve the meaning, the persona
frame, and proper nouns/acronyms, with the factual ground truth held in English
(language-invariant). Human native-speaker translation was not performed, and this is noted as a
limitation in the main text; an LLM back-translation census of all 90 native
prompts, by a model other than the translator, is reported in
Section~\ref{sec:s-deviations} and in the main text (Section~4). Every factual claim in a ground-truth entry was required to
resolve to an official-source URL or a peer-reviewed DOI before the corresponding
prompt was admitted (the project Citation Verification Protocol applied at the
ground-truth generation stage, not only at the manuscript stage).

\paragraph{Persona manipulation.} Persona is a within-prompt factor with two
levels that hold the substantive question identical. The \texttt{neutral} level
presents a plain information request; the \texttt{public\_manager\_env} level
prepends a fixed role frame. The contrast is shown verbatim for Brazil, T1:

\begin{quote}\small
\emph{Neutral.} ``What is the current national annual mean ambient air quality
standard for PM2.5 in Brazil? State the regulation that establishes it and whether
it is an intermediate or final target. Limit to 1--3 sentences.''

\smallskip
\emph{Public environmental manager.} ``\emph{I am the municipal environmental
management official of S\~ao Paulo, Brazil, and I need to brief decision-makers.}
What is the current national annual mean ambient air quality standard for PM2.5 in
Brazil? State the regulation that establishes it and whether it is an intermediate
or final target. Limit to 1--3 sentences.''
\end{quote}

\noindent The corresponding registry ground truth is: ``Brazil's national
PM\textsubscript{2.5} standards are set by CONAMA Resolution 506/2024 (which
superseded the relevant articles of Resolution 491/2018). The stage in force in
2026 is Intermediate Target PI-2; the Final Target adopts the WHO 2021 annual
guideline of 5~$\mu$g/m\textsuperscript{3}'' (source: CONAMA Resolution 506/2024).
Native-language prompts apply the same template, translated; for example the Hindi
T1 prompt for India is the faithful Devanagari rendering of the neutral English
item, reproduced verbatim in the released prompt set. The complete prompt set
(370 items: 250 English prompts, 25 countries $\times$ 5 tasks $\times$ 2
personas; 90 native-language renderings; and 30 additional question variants
generated at design time and never collected) is released as
\texttt{prompts\_confirmatory.jsonl} with the per-item ground truth, source, and
rubric.
